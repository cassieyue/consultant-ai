# McKinsey 7S Framework — Application Template

## When to use
Analyzing organizational alignment, diagnosing change management challenges, or assessing whether an organization is capable of executing a strategy. Use when: a strategy exists but isn't being executed, during mergers/acquisitions (integration planning), or when diagnosing why an organization is underperforming.

## The seven elements

The 7S framework divides organizational elements into **Hard** (easier to define and measure) and **Soft** (harder to define, but often more important for execution).

### Hard Elements

**Strategy**
- The plan for achieving competitive advantage
- What choices has the company made about where to compete and how to win?
- Is the strategy clear, differentiated, and communicated?

**Structure**
- How is the organization divided? (Functional, divisional, matrix, flat, hierarchical)
- Reporting lines and decision-making authority
- Centralized vs. decentralized

**Systems**
- The processes and procedures that govern daily operations
- Financial systems, HR systems, IT systems, performance management
- Information flows: who knows what, when?

### Soft Elements

**Shared Values** (center of the model — everything connects here)
- The core beliefs and culture of the organization
- What does the company actually stand for (not just what it says)?
- Do employees' behaviors reflect the stated values?

**Style**
- Leadership style and management culture
- How do leaders make decisions? (Collaborative vs. directive)
- What behaviors are rewarded and modeled?

**Staff**
- The people: their skills, experience, and mindsets
- How are people recruited, developed, and motivated?
- Are the right capabilities in place for the strategy?

**Skills**
- Organizational capabilities — what the company is distinctively good at
- Core competencies that create competitive advantage
- Gaps between current skills and those needed for the strategy

## How to use it

The 7S framework is an **alignment diagnostic** — the power is in spotting misalignments, not just describing each element.

**Step 1**: Describe the current state of each element.
**Step 2**: Identify the desired state (usually: what does the strategy require?).
**Step 3**: Find the gaps — where is there misalignment between elements, or between current and desired state?

The model predicts that when any of the 7S elements are misaligned, organizational performance suffers — even if the strategy is good.

## Final synthesis
Which 2-3 misalignments are most critical to fix for the strategy to succeed? Prioritize by: (1) impact on strategy execution, (2) feasibility of change.

## Common mistakes
- Describing each element without assessing alignment — a description is not an analysis
- Treating Shared Values as a soft/unimportant element — culture is often the hardest thing to change and the most important for execution
- Using the framework for competitive analysis (it's internal — use Porter's Five Forces or PESTEL for external)
