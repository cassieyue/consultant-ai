# Ansoff Matrix — Application Template

## When to use
Evaluating growth strategy options. Which direction should a company grow — deeper into existing business, or into new products/markets? Use when: a company needs a growth strategy, evaluating risk of different expansion options, or deciding where to allocate resources.

## The four quadrants

|  | Existing Products | New Products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing Markets** | Market Penetration | Product Development |
| **New Markets** | Market Development | Diversification |

### Market Penetration (lowest risk)
Grow by selling more of existing products to existing customers.
- Increase market share
- Increase purchase frequency
- Win customers from competitors
- **Risk level**: Low — you know the product and the market
- **When it works**: Market is underpenetrated, company has pricing or distribution advantages
- **What to find**: Current market share, competitive intensity, customer retention rates

### Market Development (medium risk)
Enter new markets with existing products.
- New geographies, new customer segments, new distribution channels
- **Risk level**: Medium — you know the product, but not the new market
- **When it works**: Existing product translates well across markets, home market is saturated
- **What to find**: Market attractiveness (PESTEL/CAGE), adaptations needed for new market, competitive landscape in new market

### Product Development (medium risk)
Develop new products for existing markets.
- Line extensions, new features, adjacent products
- **Risk level**: Medium — you know the market, but not how the new product will perform
- **When it works**: Strong customer relationships, R&D capability, market demand for innovation
- **What to find**: Customer unmet needs, R&D investment required, time-to-market

### Diversification (highest risk)
New products in new markets.
- Related diversification: adjacent to existing business
- Unrelated diversification: completely new territory
- **Risk level**: High — neither the product nor the market is familiar
- **When it works**: Core business is declining, company has capital and appetite for high-risk/reward
- **What to find**: Strategic rationale (why does this make sense for this company?), synergies, capability gaps

## Output structure

Evaluate which quadrant best fits the company's current situation and objectives:

> **Recommended strategy**: [Quadrant] — because [current market position] + [available resources] + [market opportunity] makes this the highest-value, appropriate-risk path.

Then address why the other quadrants were considered and rejected:
> **Market Penetration** considered but rejected because [reason — e.g., "market share is already 45% and is at the ceiling"].

## Connecting to other frameworks
- Use **PESTEL** to assess a new market before recommending Market Development
- Use **VRIO** to assess whether the company has the capabilities for Product Development
- Use **Porter's Five Forces** to assess whether Market Penetration is viable in the existing market

## Common mistakes
- Recommending Diversification without a clear strategic rationale (high risk needs strong justification)
- Treating all quadrants as equally viable — the matrix shows risk, and high risk needs proportionally higher expected return
- Not connecting to the company's actual resources and capabilities
