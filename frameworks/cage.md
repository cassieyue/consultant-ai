# CAGE Distance Framework — Application Template

## When to use
Comparing potential target markets to determine which is "closest" to the home market and therefore lower-risk for international expansion. CAGE explains why companies often fail in markets that look attractive on paper but are culturally or institutionally distant.

## The four dimensions of distance

### Cultural Distance
How different are the values, norms, and behaviors?

**Factors increasing distance**:
- Different language
- Different religion
- Different social norms and values (Hofstede dimensions diverge significantly)
- Different attitudes toward business, time, hierarchy, risk

**What to measure**:
- Language similarity (same language = near zero distance)
- Religious composition
- Hofstede score differences on each dimension
- Historical colonial relationship (often reduces distance)

**Business impact**: Affects marketing communication, management style, HR practices, negotiation, customer behavior.

### Administrative / Political Distance
How different are the legal and regulatory environments?

**Factors increasing distance**:
- No trade agreement between countries
- Weak IP protection in target market
- Political instability or corruption
- Different legal system (common law vs civil law)
- History of conflict or poor diplomatic relations

**Factors reducing distance**:
- Shared trade bloc (EU, ASEAN, NAFTA/USMCA)
- Colonial history (shared legal system)
- Currency union
- Strong bilateral investment treaties

**Business impact**: Affects entry mode (WOS possible vs. JV required), legal costs, contract enforceability, risk of expropriation.

### Geographic Distance
How physically far and accessible is the target market?

**Factors increasing distance**:
- Physical distance (km)
- Landlocked country with poor infrastructure
- Weak logistics network
- Different climate (affects supply chain for certain goods)
- Time zone gap (affects communication and coordination)

**Business impact**: Logistics cost, lead time, supply chain complexity, ability to manage operations remotely.

### Economic Distance
How different are the economic conditions?

**Factors increasing distance**:
- Large GDP per capita gap (affects product-market fit, pricing)
- Different income distribution
- Different stage of economic development (infrastructure, workforce skill)
- Different consumer preferences driven by income level

**Business impact**: Whether the product/service is affordable and relevant, pricing strategy, cost structure of operations.

Then for the chosen market, elaborate on the 1-2 dimensions with highest distance — these are the specific risks to mitigate.

## Final synthesis
CAGE is a prioritization tool, not a veto. The question is: does the attractiveness of the market (PESTEL, market size) outweigh the distance costs? State this explicitly: "Despite high cultural distance, Mauritius is chosen because its administrative and economic distance is low, legal environment is favorable, and the market has high growth potential."

## Common mistakes
- Using CAGE only to confirm the choice you already made, rather than genuinely comparing alternatives
- Treating all dimensions as equally weighted — weight by relevance to the specific business (a software company cares less about geographic distance than a manufacturer)
- Conflating CAGE with PESTEL — CAGE measures distance from home, PESTEL measures absolute attractiveness
