# Risk Matrix — Application Template

## When to use
Identifying, assessing, and prioritising risks for any decision, project, or strategy. Use when: evaluating market entry risks, stress-testing a recommendation before submission, assessing a new initiative, or when an academic brief explicitly requires risk analysis. The risk matrix does not make risks disappear — it makes them visible, prioritised, and manageable.

## Step 1 — Identify all risks

Brainstorm comprehensively before scoring. Use these categories to avoid blind spots:

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Strategic | Competitor response, market timing wrong, technology disruption, strategic misalignment |
| Operational | Process failure, quality issues, supply chain disruption, key person dependency |
| Financial | Currency fluctuation, cost overrun, pricing risk, credit risk, liquidity |
| Regulatory / Legal | Regulatory change, compliance failure, litigation, IP infringement, sanctions |
| Market / Commercial | Customer adoption lower than expected, channel conflict, partner failure |
| Reputational | Brand damage, ESG/ethics concerns, adverse media coverage |
| Macro | Political instability, economic downturn, natural disaster, pandemic (from PESTEL) |

**What to find**: Review PESTEL, Porter's Five Forces, CAGE, and Stakeholder Analysis outputs — each will surface specific risk events applicable to this decision.

## Step 2 — Score each risk

For each risk, independently score **Probability** and **Impact** on a 1–5 scale:

**Probability**:
- 1 = Very unlikely (< 10%)
- 2 = Unlikely (10–30%)
- 3 = Possible (30–60%)
- 4 = Likely (60–80%)
- 5 = Very likely (> 80%)

**Impact** (if the risk occurs):
- 1 = Negligible (minor inconvenience, no strategic effect)
- 2 = Minor (small cost or delay, recoverable)
- 3 = Moderate (significant cost, delay, or capability loss)
- 4 = Major (serious financial loss or strategic setback)
- 5 = Catastrophic (project/strategy failure, legal action, existential financial loss)

**Risk Score** = Probability × Impact (range: 1–25)

## Step 3 — Map and prioritise

| | Impact 1 | Impact 2 | Impact 3 | Impact 4 | Impact 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prob 5** | 5 | 10 | 15 | **20** | **25** |
| **Prob 4** | 4 | 8 | 12 | **16** | **20** |
| **Prob 3** | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | **15** |
| **Prob 2** | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| **Prob 1** | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

**Priority tiers**: Score **15–25** = HIGH (must mitigate) | Score **8–12** = MEDIUM (mitigate if cost-effective) | Score **1–6** = LOW (monitor)

Note: Do not ignore very-high-impact / very-low-probability risks (top-right corner). A score of 5 (P=1, I=5) is LOW priority statistically, but catastrophic impact warrants a contingency plan regardless of probability.

## Step 4 — Define mitigation for HIGH and MEDIUM risks

| Strategy | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Avoid** | Redesign the plan to eliminate the risk entirely |
| **Reduce probability** | Preventive action to make the risk less likely to occur |
| **Reduce impact** | Contingency preparation to limit consequences if the risk occurs |
| **Transfer** | Insurance, contractual risk transfer to partner or supplier |
| **Accept** | Acknowledge the risk and plan to deal with it if it materialises — only appropriate for LOW risks |

## Output structure

| Risk | Category | Probability (1–5) | Impact (1–5) | Score | Priority | Mitigation Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Description] | [Category] | | | P×I | HIGH/MED/LOW | [Specific action] |

Then for the top 3 risks by score:
> **[Risk]**: [Why this risk exists in this specific context, with evidence] → **Mitigation**: [Specific action to reduce probability or impact] → **Residual risk**: [What remains after mitigation — this is the accepted exposure]

## Common mistakes
- Scoring risks as uniformly "unlikely" to make the analysis look clean — honest scoring is the only point of the exercise
- Identifying risks without mitigation — a list of risks is observation; risk management requires a response to each HIGH and MEDIUM risk
- Treating LOW-probability, HIGH-impact risks as irrelevant — score 5 events can still be catastrophic and warrant contingency planning
- Presenting a static matrix — risk profiles change as conditions change; flag which risks should be re-assessed at specific milestones

## Combines with
- **PESTEL**: The six PESTEL dimensions are a structured risk identification tool — each Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, and Legal factor in a PESTEL analysis translates to one or more specific risk events for the matrix; run PESTEL first to populate the risk identification step
- **Modes of Entry**: Each entry mode has a fundamentally different risk profile — exporting has low financial risk and high commercial risk; WOS has high financial and operational risk; map the chosen entry mode's risks directly into the matrix
- **Stakeholder Analysis**: High-power opponents from the stakeholder map are specific HIGH-probability risks — map them as political/regulatory or commercial risk events in the matrix with appropriate mitigation strategies
