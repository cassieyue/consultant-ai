# PESTEL Analysis — Application Template

## When to use
Scanning a country or market for attractiveness, identifying macro-level risks and opportunities before entry or investment.

## What to collect per dimension

### Political
- Government stability (EIU Democracy Index, political risk ratings)
- Trade policy: tariffs, trade agreements, import/export restrictions
- Tax policy for foreign businesses
- Regulations affecting your specific industry
- Intellectual property protection strength
- Corruption level (Transparency International Corruption Perceptions Index)
- Relationship between target country and home country

**So what**: Does the political environment enable or constrain the business model?

### Economic
- GDP and GDP growth rate (World Bank)
- GDP per capita / GNI per capita (purchasing power of consumers)
- Inflation rate and trend
- Unemployment rate
- Exchange rate stability and trend
- Ease of Doing Business score (World Bank)
- FDI inflows as % of GDP (openness to foreign investment)
- Debt-to-GDP ratio (fiscal health)
- Size and growth of the specific market/industry

**So what**: Is there enough economic activity and consumer spending to support the business?

### Social
- Population size and demographics (median age, urbanization rate)
- Income distribution and middle-class size
- Cultural attitudes relevant to the product/service
- Education and literacy rates
- Language(s) spoken
- Religion and its business implications
- Consumer behavior patterns

**So what**: Is there a willing and able customer base?

### Technological
- Digital infrastructure (internet penetration, smartphone adoption)
- Telecommunications quality
- R&D spending as % of GDP
- Technology adoption rates in the target industry
- Patent and IP environment
- E-commerce maturity

**So what**: Can the business operate technically, and is there digital demand?

### Environmental
- Climate and geography (logistics implications)
- Environmental regulations
- Natural resource availability
- Sustainability expectations of consumers and regulators
- Climate risk to operations

**So what**: Are there environmental constraints or costs to factor in?

### Legal
- Business registration and foreign ownership rules (can foreigners own 100%?)
- Import/export duties and customs
- Employment law
- Consumer protection law
- Industry-specific regulations
- IP law and enforcement
- Dispute resolution mechanisms

**So what**: Can the business operate legally and protect its assets?

## Output structure

For each dimension:
> **[Dimension]**: [2-3 sentences of factual findings with data] → **Implication**: [1 sentence on what this means for the decision]

## Final synthesis
Identify the 2-3 factors that most strongly support entry and the 2-3 that most strongly argue against. Weight by their impact on the business model specifically — a high-tariff environment matters more to a manufacturing company than a service firm.

## Common mistakes
- Listing facts without drawing implications ("GDP is $X billion" is not analysis)
- Treating all factors as equally important — weight by relevance to the specific business
- Not distinguishing between current state and trend (a stable but improving environment is different from a stable but deteriorating one)
