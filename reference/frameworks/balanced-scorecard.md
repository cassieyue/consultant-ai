# Balanced Scorecard — Application Template

## When to use
Translating strategy into measurable objectives across four perspectives. Use when: a strategy is defined but not yet operationalized; assessing whether an organization is executing against its strategy; or designing a performance management system. Most powerful in strategy execution, change management, and organizational alignment contexts.

## The four perspectives

### Financial
How do we look to shareholders? (Lagging indicator — result of the other three)
- Revenue growth rate, profit margin (gross and net)
- ROE / ROA / ROIC
- Cost reduction vs. plan
- Cash flow generation, dividend yield

**Question**: What financial results must the strategy deliver, and in what timeframe?

### Customer
How do customers see us? (Lags operational improvements by 1–2 quarters)
- Net Promoter Score (NPS) or customer satisfaction (CSAT)
- Market share in target segments
- Customer retention / churn rate
- Customer acquisition cost
- Net revenue per customer

**Question**: What must we deliver to customers to produce the financial outcomes?

### Internal Processes
What must we excel at internally? (Leads customer outcomes)
- On-time delivery rate, order fulfillment cycle time
- Product defect / quality rate
- Innovation pipeline (new products per year, R&D cycle time)
- Cost per transaction or service unit

**Question**: Which internal processes must we excel at to satisfy customers and manage costs?

### Learning & Growth
How do we sustain our ability to change and improve? (Foundation — leads everything else)
- Employee engagement score
- Training hours per employee
- Key talent retention rate
- % of processes digitized or automated
- R&D investment as % of revenue

**Question**: What capabilities, skills, and systems must we build to excel at internal processes?

## Build a strategy map first

Before selecting KPIs, draw a strategy map: a cause-and-effect chain linking L&G → Internal Processes → Customer → Financial.

Example: "If we invest in employee training (L&G), we improve service quality (Internal Processes), which increases customer satisfaction (Customer), which drives revenue growth and margin expansion (Financial)."

This causal chain is the business logic the scorecard is testing. Without it, you're just measuring things, not tracking whether the strategy is working.

## Output structure

For each perspective, present objectives and KPIs:

| Perspective | Strategic Objective | KPI | Target | Current | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Financial | [e.g., Improve profitability] | Net profit margin | 15% | 11% | At risk |
| Customer | [e.g., Improve retention] | Customer churn rate | <5% | 8% | Off track |
| Internal | [e.g., Reduce delivery time] | Order fulfillment days | 3 days | 6 days | Off track |
| L&G | [e.g., Upskill workforce] | Training hours/employee | 40h/yr | 18h/yr | Off track |

Four objectives per perspective is typical — 16 KPIs total is a manageable scorecard.

Then identify the 2–3 KPIs most off-track and trace the cascading impact using the strategy map:
> **[Lagging KPI] is off-track**: Root cause traces back to [leading KPI at L&G or Internal level]. Fix: [specific action at the root cause level].

## Common mistakes
- Measuring everything rather than strategy-critical objectives — 30+ KPIs is a dashboard, not a scorecard
- Setting targets without owners — every KPI needs a named owner accountable for the result
- Skipping the strategy map — without the causal chain, you have metrics but no way to test whether the strategy logic is correct
- Treating the financial perspective as the primary goal — in the BSC model, financial results are the outcome of getting the other three perspectives right

## Combines with
- **McKinsey 7S**: Use 7S to diagnose which organizational elements (Staff, Skills, Systems) are causing the L&G and Internal Process gaps that the scorecard reveals; 7S identifies what needs to change organizationally
- **VRIO**: BSC KPIs should reflect the resources and capabilities identified as sustained competitive advantages by VRIO — if VRIO finds brand is the key advantage, customer perception KPIs should be prominent in the Customer perspective
- **Financial Ratio Analysis**: Financial perspective KPIs align directly with ratio analysis — use industry ratio benchmarks to set realistic and comparative targets for the financial perspective
