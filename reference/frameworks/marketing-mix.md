# Marketing Mix (4Ps / 7Ps) — Application Template

## When to use
Planning the go-to-market approach for a product or service: what to offer, at what price, through which channels, and with what messaging. Always apply after STP — the mix operationalizes the positioning. Use 4Ps for product businesses; 7Ps adds People, Process, and Physical Evidence for service businesses.

## The four Ps (all businesses)

### Product
What is being offered and what need does it serve?
- **Core product**: the functional benefit (what the customer is actually buying)
- **Actual product**: features, quality level, design, brand name, packaging
- **Augmented product**: warranty, after-sales service, delivery, financing
- **Product lifecycle stage**: Introduction / Growth / Maturity / Decline — affects every other Mix decision

**What to find**: Customer needs for target segment (from STP), competitor product features, product differentiation opportunities

### Price
What is the pricing strategy and how does it signal value?

| Strategy | Logic | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Cost-plus | Cost × markup | When pricing power is limited, commodity products |
| Competitive | Price relative to competitors | Undifferentiated market, reactive strategy |
| Value-based | Price = perceived value to customer | Differentiated products with strong brand |
| Penetration | Below market to gain share fast | New market entry, high price elasticity |
| Premium / skimming | High initial price, lower over time | Innovative products, prestige positioning |
| Freemium | Basic free, paid for advanced features | Platform businesses, SaaS |

**What to find**: Willingness to pay in target segment, competitor pricing, cost structure and minimum margin floor

### Place (Distribution)
How does the product reach the customer?
- **Direct channels**: own stores, own website, own sales force (high control, high cost)
- **Indirect channels**: distributors, retailers, agents, marketplaces (lower cost, lower control)
- **Distribution intensity**: Intensive (everywhere) / Selective (chosen retailers) / Exclusive (one per region)
- **Channel conflict risk**: Selling through multiple channels at different prices or conditions creates tension

**What to find**: Where target customers shop or buy, competitor channels, margin structure of each channel option

### Promotion
How does the company communicate the value proposition to the target segment?
- **Push strategy** (trade promotions, B2B sales): incentivize the channel to stock and sell
- **Pull strategy** (advertising, content, social): build consumer demand so customers ask for the product
- **Promotional mix elements**: Advertising, PR, Sales promotion, Personal selling, Direct marketing, Digital/Social

Message must align with positioning from STP — if you are positioned as premium, discounting undermines the strategy.

**What to find**: Where target customers seek information, competitor messaging, promotional budget available

## The additional 3Ps (service businesses)

### People
The employees who deliver the service. Their skills, appearance, attitude, and behaviors shape customer perception at every touchpoint.
- **What to assess**: Hiring and training standards, service consistency across staff, alignment with brand values

### Process
The systems and procedures used to deliver the service.
- Booking and onboarding, service delivery steps, quality checks, complaint handling
- **What to assess**: How standardized and consistent is service delivery? Where are failure points?

### Physical Evidence
Tangible cues that help customers evaluate an intangible service.
- Store design, uniforms, website quality, certificates, customer reviews, packaging
- **What to assess**: Do physical cues consistently reinforce the positioning and quality signal?

## Output structure

For each P, state what the company should do and why:
> **[P]**: [Specific choice] → **Rationale**: [Why this fits the target segment and positioning from STP]

Then assess internal consistency: Do all Ps reinforce the same positioning? A premium-positioned product sold through discount channels, or a value brand priced at a premium, is a Mix inconsistency that needs resolution.

## Common mistakes
- Applying the Mix before defining STP — the Mix is meaningless without a defined target segment and positioning
- Treating Price as just a number rather than a strategic signal — pricing communicates value and affects brand perception
- Ignoring channel conflict — selling through multiple channels at different prices creates tension and damages brand
- Describing the current Mix only without recommending improvements

## Combines with
- **STP**: Always run STP first — target segment and positioning define every Mix choice; the Mix operationalizes the positioning statement
- **SWOT**: Mix recommendations should leverage Strengths identified in SWOT and address Weaknesses; SO strategies from TOWS often become specific Mix actions
- **Value Chain**: Mix decisions (especially Place) require specific value chain activities to execute — channel choice affects operations, procurement, and outbound logistics activities
