# SCOR Model — Application Template

## When to use
Analyzing, benchmarking, or improving a supply chain. Use when: diagnosing supply chain performance, identifying bottlenecks, comparing a company's supply chain to industry standards, or recommending supply chain improvements.

## The six process categories

### 1. Plan
Balancing supply and demand across the supply chain.
- Demand forecasting accuracy
- Inventory planning and safety stock levels
- Capacity planning
- Supply chain risk planning

**What to assess**: How accurately does the company forecast demand? How is excess inventory or stockouts managed?

### 2. Source
Procurement and supplier management.
- Supplier selection criteria and process
- Number of suppliers (concentrated vs. diversified)
- Supplier performance management
- Procurement cost as % of COGS
- Lead times from suppliers

**What to assess**: Is the company over-reliant on single suppliers? Are procurement costs competitive? How are supplier relationships managed?

### 3. Make
Manufacturing and production.
- Production cycle time
- Capacity utilization
- Quality defect rates
- Make-to-stock vs. make-to-order
- Manufacturing flexibility

**What to assess**: Is production efficient? Can the company respond to demand spikes? Where are quality issues arising?

### 4. Deliver
Order management, logistics, and distribution.
- On-time delivery rate
- Order fulfillment cycle time
- Logistics cost as % of revenue
- Distribution network design
- Last-mile delivery

**What to assess**: How reliably does the company deliver to customers? What is the cost-to-serve?

### 5. Return
Reverse logistics — handling returns, recalls, and repairs.
- Return processing time
- Cost of returns as % of revenue
- Refurbishment and resale rates
- Customer satisfaction with return process

**What to assess**: Is the returns process a cost center or a competitive differentiator (e.g., easy returns as a loyalty driver)?

### 6. Enable
Cross-cutting capabilities that support the other five processes.
- Supply chain visibility and data quality
- Technology systems (ERP, WMS, TMS)
- Workforce skills and training
- Risk management and business continuity

**What to assess**: Does the company have the systems and capabilities to manage its supply chain effectively?

## Key performance metrics

| Metric | Category | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect Order Fulfillment | Deliver | % of orders delivered on time, complete, undamaged, with correct documentation |
| Order Fulfillment Cycle Time | Deliver | Time from customer order to delivery |
| Supply Chain Flexibility | Plan/Make | How fast can output change in response to demand shifts? |
| Supply Chain Cost | All | Total supply chain cost as % of revenue |
| Cash-to-Cash Cycle Time | All | Days from paying suppliers to collecting from customers |
| Return on Supply Chain Fixed Assets | Enable | Return generated on supply chain infrastructure |

## Output structure

For each of the 6 process areas, assess:
> **[Process]**: [Current state — what the company does and how well] → **Gap**: [Where performance falls short vs. best practice or competitors] → **Implication**: [Impact on cost, service, or competitive position]

## Final synthesis
Identify the 2-3 highest-priority improvement areas. Quantify the potential impact where possible ("reducing order fulfillment cycle time from 7 days to 4 days would enable same-week delivery and reduce safety stock by ~15%").

## Common mistakes
- Describing supply chain activities without assessing performance against a standard
- Recommending technology solutions without first diagnosing whether the problem is process, people, or systems
- Ignoring the Enable layer — poor data quality or systems undermine every other process
