# BCG Growth-Share Matrix — Application Template

## When to use
Portfolio management: deciding how to allocate resources across multiple business units, products, or markets. Use when: a company has multiple distinct products/divisions and needs to decide where to invest, maintain, harvest, or divest.

## The four quadrants

Axes:
- X-axis: **Relative market share** (vs. largest competitor) — proxy for competitive strength
- Y-axis: **Market growth rate** — proxy for market attractiveness

|  | High Market Share | Low Market Share |
|---|---|---|
| **High Growth** | Stars | Question Marks |
| **Low Growth** | Cash Cows | Dogs |

### Stars (High share, High growth)
- Leaders in fast-growing markets
- Generate significant cash but also consume it (investment needed to maintain leadership)
- **Strategic priority**: Invest to maintain position; they become Cash Cows as growth slows
- **What to find**: Market growth rate, company's market share vs. competitors

### Cash Cows (High share, Low growth)
- Dominant position in mature markets
- Generate more cash than they consume — fund the rest of the portfolio
- **Strategic priority**: Milk them efficiently; avoid over-investing, but don't neglect
- **What to find**: Cash generation, margin levels, rate of market maturity

### Question Marks (Low share, High growth)
- Operating in fast-growing markets but not yet leaders
- Consume cash, uncertain future
- **Strategic decision**: Invest to become a Star (requires significant resources) OR divest
- **What to find**: What it would cost to become a leader, probability of success, competitive landscape

### Dogs (Low share, Low growth)
- Weak position in stagnant markets
- May be cash neutral or draining resources
- **Strategic priority**: Divest or harvest (extract remaining cash) unless there's a niche strategy
- **What to find**: Whether it's breakeven or a cash drain, strategic importance to other units

## How to calculate positions

**Relative market share** = Company's market share ÷ Largest competitor's market share
- If company has 25% share and leader has 50%: relative share = 0.5 (Question Mark territory)
- If company IS the leader with 50% and next has 25%: relative share = 2.0 (Star/Cash Cow)

**Market growth rate**: Use industry CAGR data (Statista, IBISWorld, industry reports)
- Threshold for "high growth" is typically 10% (or use the industry average as the dividing line)

## Output structure

For each business unit/product:
> **[Unit]**: [Quadrant] — Market share = X% vs. leader's Y% (relative share = Z). Market growing at X% CAGR. → **Recommendation**: [Invest / Maintain / Harvest / Divest] because [reason].

Then state the portfolio-level implication: Is the portfolio balanced? Are the Cash Cows funding enough Stars? Are there too many Dogs?

## Common mistakes
- Applying BCG to a single-product company (it's a portfolio tool — you need multiple units to compare)
- Not calculating relative market share (just "our share is 20%" is insufficient)
- Taking the quadrant labels as definitive verdicts — "Dog" doesn't automatically mean divest; some Dogs are strategically important for other reasons
- Confusing market growth rate with company revenue growth
