# MECE Structuring — Application Template

## What it is
MECE = Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive.
- **Mutually Exclusive**: No overlap between categories — each idea belongs in exactly one bucket
- **Collectively Exhaustive**: Together the categories cover everything — nothing is left out

It is the foundational structuring principle in consulting. Every issue tree, slide structure, recommendation, and framework application should be MECE.

## Why it matters
Non-MECE thinking leads to:
- Double-counting (same idea appears twice with different labels)
- Missing gaps (important considerations left out)
- Unclear logic (reader can't follow the argument)
- Weak recommendations (the structure doesn't actually address the full problem)

## How to build a MECE structure

### Step 1: State the question clearly
"What should X do about Y?" — vague questions produce non-MECE structures.

### Step 2: Choose a structuring approach
Pick one of these proven ways to divide a problem:

**By component** (what are the parts?):
- Revenue = Volume × Price
- Profit = Revenue - Costs
- Market share = Company sales / Total market sales
- Costs = Fixed costs + Variable costs

**By segment** (who are the different groups?):
- Customer segments (B2B vs B2C)
- Geography (North America, Europe, Asia)
- Product lines

**By process / timeline** (what are the stages?):
- Enter → Operate → Exit
- Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Retention
- Plan → Source → Make → Deliver

**By hypothesis** (what could explain this?):
- "Revenue declined because: (A) volume fell, OR (B) price fell, OR (C) mix shifted"
- These are mutually exclusive causes — testing each one narrows down the diagnosis

### Step 3: Test for MECE
1. Can any item appear in two buckets? → Not ME (fix by clarifying definitions)
2. Is there anything that doesn't fit in any bucket? → Not CE (fix by adding a bucket or restructuring)
3. Do the buckets together fully answer the question? → If not, CE is broken

### Step 4: Build the next level
Each bucket becomes a sub-question. Apply the same MECE test at each level.

## Common MECE structures

**Diagnosing a business problem**:
- Is it an internal issue or external issue?
  - Internal: people, process, technology, or strategy?
  - External: market, competitive, regulatory, or macro?

**Market sizing**:
- Top-down: Total addressable market → penetration rate → your share
- Bottom-up: Number of customers × purchase frequency × price

**Profitability problem**:
- Revenue down or costs up?
  - Revenue: volume or price?
  - Costs: fixed or variable? Which cost line?

## What non-MECE looks like (and how to fix)

| Non-MECE | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "Our issues are: customers, competition, and lack of customers" | "Customers" and "lack of customers" overlap | Replace with: "demand-side" and "supply-side" issues |
| "We should focus on marketing, sales, and online sales" | "Online sales" is a subset of "sales" | Separate by channel instead: offline vs. online |
| "The problem is cost, quality, and pricing" | "Pricing" affects both revenue and cost — not ME | Restructure as: revenue-side vs. cost-side |

## Applying to a slide deck

Every slide deck should be MECE at the top level:
- Each slide covers a distinct, non-overlapping topic
- Together the slides tell the complete story needed to support the recommendation
- No slide is redundant; no critical point is missing

## Common mistakes
- Using generic buckets that aren't actually MECE ("pros and cons" is often not CE — it may miss implementation issues)
- Treating MECE as a box-ticking exercise rather than a thinking discipline
- Building 2 levels of MECE tree and stopping — go 3 levels to get to specific, testable claims
