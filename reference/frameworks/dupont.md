# DuPont Analysis — Application Template

## When to use
Decomposing Return on Equity (ROE) to identify the specific operational and financial drivers behind a company's profitability. Use when: diagnosing why ROE is high or low, benchmarking against competitors, identifying which management lever to pull for improvement, or understanding a company's financial strategy and risk profile.

## The DuPont decomposition

### 3-factor DuPont (standard)

**ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage**

| Factor | Formula | What it measures | Business model implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net Profit Margin | Net Income ÷ Revenue | Profitability per dollar of sales | Pricing power and cost discipline |
| Asset Turnover | Revenue ÷ Total Assets | Revenue generated per dollar of assets | Capital efficiency and operational intensity |
| Financial Leverage | Total Assets ÷ Shareholders' Equity | How much debt amplifies equity returns | Capital structure and risk appetite |

**Interpreting the combination**:
- High margin + Low turnover = Premium / differentiated strategy (pharmaceuticals, luxury goods, software)
- Low margin + High turnover = Volume / efficiency model (retail, FMCG, logistics, airlines)
- High leverage = ROE is amplified but fragile — must stress-test against earnings volatility

Two companies with identical ROE may be fundamentally different businesses: one earns it through pricing power, the other through capital efficiency, the third through leverage.

### 5-factor DuPont (extended)

**ROE = Tax Burden × Interest Burden × EBIT Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier**

| Factor | Formula |
|---|---|
| Tax Burden | Net Income ÷ EBT |
| Interest Burden | EBT ÷ EBIT |
| EBIT Margin | EBIT ÷ Revenue |
| Asset Turnover | Revenue ÷ Total Assets |
| Equity Multiplier | Total Assets ÷ Equity |

**When to use the 5-factor version**: When comparing companies in different tax environments, different countries, or with very different debt structures — the 5-factor version isolates operating performance (EBIT margin + asset turnover) from financing and tax decisions.

## How to calculate

1. Gather 3–5 years of income statement and balance sheet data for the company
2. Gather the same for 2–3 competitors and an industry average
3. Calculate each DuPont component for each year and each company
4. Build the comparison table

## Output structure

| Factor | Company (Yr 1) | Company (Yr 2) | Company (Yr 3) | Competitor A | Industry Avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net Profit Margin | X% | | | | |
| Asset Turnover | X.Xx | | | | |
| Financial Leverage | X.Xx | | | | |
| **ROE** | **X%** | | | | |

Then for each factor that explains a significant difference vs. competitors or a notable trend over time:
> **[Factor]**: Company is at [X] vs. competitor average of [Y]. This is because [specific explanation — e.g., higher SG&A as % of revenue, lower asset base due to asset-light model]. The implication is [strategic conclusion — e.g., the company is trading asset efficiency for operational flexibility].

## Final synthesis
State: What is driving the company's ROE — is it margin, efficiency, leverage, or a combination? Is this ROE level sustainable and strategically appropriate for this industry? If ROE is below competitors, which single DuPont factor explains the largest gap and what would it take to close it?

## Common mistakes
- Using DuPont without benchmarks — a 12% ROE means nothing without comparison to competitors and industry norms
- Ignoring the leverage component — a high ROE driven entirely by high debt is fragile; always stress-test: what happens to ROE if borrowing costs rise 2%, or if EBIT falls 20%?
- Over-interpreting single-year results — calculate 3–5 years to distinguish structural performance from one-time events

## Combines with
- **Financial Ratio Analysis**: DuPont drills down specifically on ROE, one ratio in the ratio analysis; run ratio analysis first for full context (profitability, liquidity, leverage, efficiency), then use DuPont to explain the profitability dynamics in detail
- **BCG Matrix**: DuPont validates BCG portfolio positioning — Cash Cows should show stable high margin and steady asset turnover; Stars should show improving margins as scale builds; Dogs will show eroding margins
- **Balanced Scorecard**: DuPont components map to BSC perspectives — Net Margin to Financial, Asset Turnover to Internal Processes (operational efficiency), Leverage to Financial risk management
