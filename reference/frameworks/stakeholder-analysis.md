# Stakeholder Analysis — Application Template

## When to use
Identifying and assessing all parties who can affect or be affected by a decision, strategy, or project. Use when: planning a market entry, managing a change initiative, launching a new product, or navigating a complex regulatory environment. Helps anticipate resistance, build a coalition of support, and design targeted engagement strategies.

## Step 1 — Identify all stakeholders

Cast a wide net before narrowing. Stakeholder categories:

- **Internal**: shareholders/board, senior management, employees, unions
- **Commercial**: customers (key accounts and segments), suppliers, distributors, partners, competitors
- **Regulatory**: government bodies, sector regulators, standards bodies, tax authorities, customs
- **External**: local communities, NGOs, media, industry associations, academic institutions
- **International** (for cross-border decisions): foreign governments, embassies, multilateral organizations (World Bank, UN agencies)

**What to find**: Org charts, regulatory filings, press coverage, industry association membership, government consultation responses

## Step 2 — Assess each stakeholder on three dimensions

**Interest**: How much does this stakeholder care about the outcome?
- What do they stand to gain or lose from the decision?
- Distinguish stated interests (what they say) from actual interests (what drives their behavior)

**Power/Influence**: How much can this stakeholder affect the outcome?
- Formal authority: regulator, government ministry, legal authority
- Informal influence: media, community leaders, respected NGOs
- Economic leverage: major customer, critical supplier, dominant investor

**Position**: Supporter / Neutral / Opponent
- Assess based on evidence (what they have said or done) — do not assume position

Rate each: High / Medium / Low for Interest and Power.

## Step 3 — Map to the power-interest grid

| | Low Interest | High Interest |
|---|---|---|
| **High Power** | Keep satisfied | Manage closely |
| **Low Power** | Monitor | Keep informed |

## Step 4 — Engagement strategy per quadrant

**Manage closely** (High Power + High Interest): Regular two-way communication, involve in planning or consultation where appropriate, address concerns proactively, track position changes

**Keep satisfied** (High Power + Low Interest): Regular updates, no surprises, brief them before announcements — avoid raising their interest unnecessarily

**Keep informed** (Low Power + High Interest): One-way communication, public consultation, newsletters, Q&A sessions

**Monitor** (Low Power + Low Interest): Passive monitoring; respond if they shift position or coalitions form

## Output structure

| Stakeholder | Interest | Power | Position | Quadrant | Engagement Strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Name/group] | H/M/L | H/M/L | Supporter / Neutral / Opponent | Manage closely / Keep satisfied / Keep informed / Monitor | [Specific action — who contacts them, how often, with what message] |

Then for the top 3–5 stakeholders in "Manage closely":
> **[Stakeholder]**: [What they care about specifically] → [Key risk if not managed] → [Engagement plan: who, how, when, and what message]

## Common mistakes
- Conflating power and interest — a community group may care deeply (high interest) but have limited real ability to block a decision
- Only listing formal stakeholders — informal influence (media, influential individuals) is often underweighted
- Treating stakeholder positions as fixed — positions shift in response to new information, compensation offers, coalition building, or media coverage
- Not revisiting the analysis as the situation evolves

## Combines with
- **CAGE Distance Framework**: In international contexts, CAGE's Administrative dimension maps directly to regulatory and political stakeholder complexity — use CAGE to understand which stakeholder relationships are most different from what the company knows at home
- **McKinsey 7S**: Internal stakeholders (staff, leadership) are central to the Style and Staff elements of 7S; organizational change initiatives must account for employee and management stakeholder positions
- **Risk Matrix**: Stakeholder opposition is a risk category — map high-power opponents directly onto the risk matrix as probability × impact events and define mitigation strategies for each
