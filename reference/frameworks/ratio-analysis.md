# Financial Ratio Analysis — Application Template

## When to use
Diagnosing a company's financial health, benchmarking against competitors, or assessing investment attractiveness. Use when: evaluating company performance, identifying financial risks, or understanding the drivers of profitability.

## The five categories

### 1. Profitability Ratios
*Is the company generating adequate returns?*

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Gross Profit Margin | Gross Profit / Revenue | Pricing power and production efficiency |
| Operating Profit Margin | Operating Income / Revenue | Operational efficiency before financing |
| Net Profit Margin | Net Income / Revenue | Bottom-line profitability |
| Return on Assets (ROA) | Net Income / Total Assets | How efficiently assets generate profit |
| Return on Equity (ROE) | Net Income / Shareholders' Equity | Return generated for shareholders |

**Benchmarks**: Compare to industry average and key competitors. A standalone number means nothing.

### 2. Liquidity Ratios
*Can the company meet short-term obligations?*

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Current Ratio | Current Assets / Current Liabilities | Short-term solvency (>1 = can pay bills) |
| Quick Ratio | (Current Assets - Inventory) / Current Liabilities | More conservative liquidity test |
| Cash Ratio | Cash & Equivalents / Current Liabilities | Strictest liquidity measure |

**Red flags**: Current ratio below 1.0, declining trend over multiple periods.

### 3. Leverage / Solvency Ratios
*How much debt is the company carrying?*

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Debt-to-Equity | Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity | Financial risk; how leveraged is the company |
| Debt-to-Assets | Total Debt / Total Assets | What % of assets are financed by debt |
| Interest Coverage | EBIT / Interest Expense | Can earnings cover interest payments? (<1.5 = danger zone) |

**Context**: High leverage isn't always bad — it depends on industry norms and stability of earnings.

### 4. Efficiency Ratios
*How well is the company using its assets?*

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Asset Turnover | Revenue / Total Assets | Revenue generated per dollar of assets |
| Inventory Turnover | COGS / Average Inventory | How fast inventory is sold |
| Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) | Accounts Receivable / (Revenue / 365) | How long to collect payment from customers |
| Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) | Accounts Payable / (COGS / 365) | How long company takes to pay suppliers |

**Working capital insight**: Lower DSO + higher DPO = better cash flow management.

### 5. Market / Valuation Ratios (for public companies)
*How is the market pricing the company?*

| Ratio | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| P/E Ratio | Stock Price / EPS | How much investors pay per dollar of earnings |
| EV/EBITDA | Enterprise Value / EBITDA | Industry-neutral valuation multiple |
| Price-to-Book | Stock Price / Book Value per Share | Premium over accounting value |

## Output structure

Present ratios in a comparison table (company vs. competitors vs. industry average vs. prior year):

| Ratio | Company | Competitor A | Industry Avg | Prior Year | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net Margin | 8.2% | 6.1% | 7.5% | 7.9% | Improving |
| ... | | | | | |

Then for each category:
> **[Category]**: [1-2 sentence interpretation] → **Implication**: [what this means for the business situation or decision]

## Final synthesis
State: What are the 2-3 most significant financial findings? Are they strengths to leverage or weaknesses to address? How do they affect the strategic recommendation?

## Common mistakes
- Presenting ratios without benchmarks — a 15% ROE is good or bad depending on the industry
- Calculating one year in isolation — trends over 3-5 years are more meaningful
- Treating accounting numbers as truth — consider the quality of earnings (are margins inflated by one-time items?)
