# Hofstede's Cultural Dimensions — Application Template

## When to use
Analyzing cultural distance between a home country and a target market. Used to anticipate management challenges, consumer behavior differences, and communication style adjustments.

## Data source
Hofstede Insights (hofstede-insights.com) — look up scores for both countries and compare.

## The six dimensions

### Power Distance Index (PDI)
**What it measures**: How much less powerful members of society accept unequal power distribution.
- High PDI (e.g. 70+): Hierarchical organizations. Employees expect to be told what to do. Decisions flow top-down. Titles and seniority matter.
- Low PDI (e.g. 30-): Flat organizations. Employees expect to be consulted. Managers accessible. Questioning authority is acceptable.

**Business implication**: Management structure, communication style, decision-making process.

### Individualism vs. Collectivism (IDV)
**What it measures**: Whether people primarily identify with themselves or their group.
- High IDV: Personal achievement valued. Contracts with individuals. Direct communication. Privacy important.
- Low IDV (Collectivist): Group harmony prioritized. Relationships before business. Indirect communication. Public criticism avoided.

**Business implication**: HR practices, negotiation style, marketing messaging, team management.

### Masculinity vs. Femininity (MAS)
**What it measures**: Competitiveness and assertiveness vs. caring and quality of life.
- High MAS: Competition valued. Performance matters. Work to live = wrong. Ambition celebrated.
- Low MAS: Work-life balance prioritized. Consensus-seeking. Caring for others valued.

**Business implication**: Workplace culture, work hours expectations, what motivates employees, marketing tone.

### Uncertainty Avoidance Index (UAI)
**What it measures**: Tolerance for ambiguity and the unknown.
- High UAI: Strong preference for rules, structure, and certainty. Risk-averse. Resistance to change.
- Low UAI: Comfortable with ambiguity. Entrepreneurial. Open to innovation.

**Business implication**: Contract specificity, decision-making speed, appetite for new products/services, bureaucracy.

### Long-Term Orientation (LTO)
**What it measures**: Focus on future rewards vs. present and past.
- High LTO: Investment in the future. Thrift and perseverance valued. Relationships built slowly.
- Low LTO (Short-term): Quick results expected. Tradition respected. Immediate gratification.

**Business implication**: Partnership timeline expectations, consumer savings vs. spending, investment horizon.

### Indulgence vs. Restraint (IVR)
**What it measures**: Degree to which people suppress or gratify desires.
- High IVR: Leisure valued. Consumer spending on enjoyment. Positive attitudes.
- Low IVR (Restraint): Strict social norms. Leisure feels guilty. More pessimistic outlook.

**Business implication**: Consumer spending on discretionary goods, marketing tone, leisure industry viability.

## Output structure

Present as a comparison table (Home Country vs. Target Country) with scores for each dimension. Then for each dimension where the gap is significant (>20 points):

> **[Dimension]**: Home = [score], Target = [score] → [what this gap means for managing people and doing business in the target market]

## Final synthesis
Identify the 2-3 dimensions where the gap is largest. These are where the company will face the most friction — in management, marketing, or operations. State specific adaptations the company should make.

## Common mistakes
- Describing each score in isolation without comparing to the home country (the gap is what matters)
- Treating scores as deterministic — they describe tendencies, not individual behavior
- Applying cultural generalizations to business decisions without connecting to the specific industry or consumer base

## Combines with
- **CAGE Distance Framework**: Hofstede scores provide the quantitative evidence for CAGE's Cultural distance dimension; feed dimension gaps (PDI, IDV, MAS, UAI, LTO) directly into the CAGE cultural distance assessment rather than estimating cultural distance qualitatively
- **Modes of Entry**: High cultural distance — especially high Power Distance or Uncertainty Avoidance in the target market — pushes toward higher-control entry modes (JV with culturally fluent local partner or WOS with local management team); low distance supports lighter-touch entry
- **STP**: Hofstede dimensions shape customer preferences, communication style, and buying behavior in the target market — IDV/COL affects how to structure marketing messages (individual benefit vs. social proof); PDI affects distribution channel and sales relationship preferences
