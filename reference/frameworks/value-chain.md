# Value Chain Analysis — Application Template

## When to use
Identifying where a company creates (or destroys) value, diagnosing cost structures, finding sources of competitive advantage, and evaluating outsourcing decisions. Use when: understanding why margins are high or low, identifying which activities to invest in vs. outsource, or connecting internal capabilities to competitive position.

## Primary activities
Activities directly involved in creating, delivering, and supporting the product or service.

### Inbound Logistics
Receiving, storing, and distributing inputs from suppliers.
- Supplier relationships and procurement efficiency
- Inventory management and storage costs
- Inbound quality control
- **What to assess**: Lead times, input quality, storage costs, supplier reliability and concentration risk

### Operations
Transforming inputs into finished products or services.
- Manufacturing processes, quality control, equipment utilization
- Make-to-stock vs. make-to-order flexibility
- **What to assess**: Production efficiency (cost per unit vs. competitors), capacity utilization, defect rates

### Outbound Logistics
Collecting, storing, and distributing outputs to customers.
- Warehousing, order fulfillment, distribution network design
- **What to assess**: On-time delivery rate, logistics cost as % of revenue, channel structure and cost-to-serve

### Marketing & Sales
Generating customer demand and enabling purchase.
- Brand strength, pricing power, advertising and promotion, sales force
- **What to assess**: Customer acquisition cost, pricing premium vs. competitors, channel efficiency

### Service
Maintaining and enhancing the product's value post-sale.
- Customer support, warranty, repair, training, self-service tools
- **What to assess**: Customer satisfaction, cost-to-serve, service as competitive differentiator vs. cost burden

## Support activities
Activities that enable and improve the primary activities across the entire organization.

### Firm Infrastructure
General management, finance, legal, quality management, planning.
- **What to assess**: Decision-making quality, governance effectiveness, financial management discipline

### Human Resource Management
Recruiting, training, compensation, retention, culture.
- **What to assess**: Talent quality vs. competitors, retention rates, alignment between culture and strategy

### Technology Development
R&D, process improvement, automation, digital systems.
- **What to assess**: Innovation pipeline, technology advantage, digitalization maturity, R&D investment as % of revenue

### Procurement
Purchasing inputs used across all primary activities — not just for production.
- **What to assess**: Procurement cost efficiency, supplier negotiating leverage, TCO vs. unit price focus

## Final synthesis
Identify: (1) which 1–2 activities are the primary sources of competitive advantage (invest and protect), (2) which activities are cost burdens with no differentiation (candidates for outsourcing), (3) how the overall value chain configuration supports or undermines the strategy.

## Common mistakes
- Describing activities without assessing them — "the company manufactures products" is not analysis
- Ignoring support activities — HRM and Technology Development are often where sustainable advantage lives
- Not connecting the analysis to a strategic conclusion: the point is to explain cost position and differentiation, not to list what the company does

## Combines with
- **VRIO**: Use value chain to identify which activities are critical; then apply VRIO to test whether those activities produce sustained competitive advantage (Valuable, Rare, Inimitable, Organized)
- **SCOR Model**: SCOR provides a detailed analysis of the supply chain portion of the value chain (Inbound Logistics + Operations + Outbound Logistics) with standardized performance metrics
- **Make-vs-Buy**: Value chain analysis identifies which activities are strategic vs. commodity — those are the make-vs-buy decisions; strategic activities should be made in-house
- **SWOT**: Value chain audit directly populates SWOT's Strengths and Weaknesses — activities with cost or quality advantage → Strengths; inefficient or undifferentiated activities → Weaknesses
