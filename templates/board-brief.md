# Board Brief Template

Use when the deliverable is a board briefing, steering committee update, or executive decision paper. Strict 1-page format. Every word earns its place — board members read in under 3 minutes.

## When to use
- Seeking board approval for a significant decision
- Updating a steering committee on a strategic initiative
- Requesting resources or sign-off from senior leadership
- Escalating a decision that requires executive authority

---

## Structure

```
BOARD BRIEF
[Project / Initiative Name]                          [Date]
Prepared by: [Name / Team]        For decision by: [Date]
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SITUATION
[2–3 sentences. What is happening? What has changed or what
opportunity/risk has emerged? No analysis yet — just the facts
a board member needs to understand the context.]

DECISION REQUIRED
[One sentence. What specific action or approval is being sought?
Be precise: "Approve $X investment in Y" not "Consider options for Y".]

RECOMMENDATION
[The recommendation, stated in one sentence.]

[2–3 supporting bullets — the strongest evidence for the recommendation.
Each bullet = one reason + the key data point that supports it.]
• [Reason 1] — [specific data or evidence]
• [Reason 2] — [specific data or evidence]
• [Reason 3] — [specific data or evidence]

FINANCIAL IMPACT
[Table or 2–3 lines. Investment required, expected return,
payback period, key financial assumptions. Include ranges, not
false precision: "$8M–$12M capex, payback 3–4 years at base case."]

| Item                | Value          | Notes               |
|---------------------|----------------|---------------------|
| Investment required | $[X]           | [one-time / annual] |
| Expected return     | $[X] / [X]%    | [timeframe]         |
| Payback period      | [X] years      | [assumptions]       |

TOP RISKS
[2–3 risks only — the ones a board member would ask about.
Each: risk in one line + mitigation in one line.]
1. [Risk] — Mitigation: [action]
2. [Risk] — Mitigation: [action]
3. [Risk] — Mitigation: [action]

THE ASK
[Restate the specific decision or approval needed.
If multiple approvals, list them:
□ Approve [X]
□ Authorise [Y]
□ Note and challenge [Z]]

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[Footer: document classification, version, contact for questions]
```

---

## Writing standards

- **Lead with the decision, not the background** — the board assumes you've done the work; get to the ask
- **Every number needs a unit and a timeframe**: "$12M" → "$12M capex in Year 1"
- **Risks are real, not performative** — list the risks that could actually change the board's decision, not the ones that make the brief look thorough
- **One recommendation** — never present options and let the board decide; that's the team's job; bring a recommendation and defend it
- **No acronyms without definition** — board members may not be in the operational detail
- **Maximum 1 page** — if it doesn't fit, the analysis isn't sharp enough yet

---

## Common mistakes
- Writing a narrative report in briefing format — cut everything that isn't situation, recommendation, evidence, financials, or risk
- Underselling the ask — state clearly what approval you need; vague asks get vague answers
- Listing every risk — pick the 2–3 that matter to this audience; exhaustive risk lists signal the team hasn't prioritised
